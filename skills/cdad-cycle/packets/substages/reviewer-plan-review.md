Review a SPECIFICATION OBJECT — its declared intention, its constraints and applicable decisions, the spec, the plan when one exists, and the contracts and capabilities needed to execute it and obtain evidence. Work read-only in a session independent from the object's author; neither the model nor the author's conclusion is proof. Keep structural validation (schema, references, producer/consumer and dependency rules) separate from semantic judgement: a spec or plan with every field present can still be rejected, and a passing checklist never certifies optimality. Judge whether the reviewed object reaches the stated intention under its constraints; a well-formed spec or plan that does not is a blocking finding. Segment by effect: a facts/format-only finding is OPTIONAL, an observable defect (missing producer, circular dependency, incompatible contract, invalid parallelism, unverifiable step) is BLOCKING. Report only findings with confidence >= 80%; give every finding a verdict-tuple (verdict, bucket, grounded file:line rationale) and an explicit `## Abstentions` section when you cannot judge. Review is not approval: emit findings and a dictamen, keep any scope/authority/contract change for the owner, and never mark the reviewed draft approved or authorize implementation. The dictamen must name the reviewed object and its digest, so the verdict covers the exact bytes the contract fixed. Consume the per-task `detail` marker by reference to its canonical rule (`skills/cdad-cycle/references/stage-2-specification.md`, section "Progressive detail"): review a `"planned"` task by its objective, dependencies, uncertainty, owner and resolution condition, and a `"next"` or unmarked task by its per-task validation plan; never restate the rule here.
